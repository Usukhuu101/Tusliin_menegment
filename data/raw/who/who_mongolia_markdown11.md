## 1. Preventing Violence Against Children (Global Commitments)
**Source:** WHO Mongolia  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/mongolia/news/detail-wpro/17-11-2026-governments-make-over-230-new-pledges-to-prevent-violence-against-children  

Governments are stepping up action to protect children from violence, with over 230 new pledges to be announced at the Second Global Ministerial Conference on Ending Violence Against Children in Manila, Philippines taking place on 18-19 November, the World Health Organization (WHO) said today.

New pledges include Tanzania’s planned US$ 190 million investment in child protection; Nepal’s pledge to halve school violence by 2030, benefiting 4.2 million children; Chile’s commitment to protect more than 3.4 million children from school violence and cyberbullying; and Mexico’s commitment to strengthen online protection by 2030. Burundi has also pledged to ban corporal punishment by 2028, while Côte d’Ivoire aims to reduce violence against children by 30% by 2030.

The commitments, including first-time pledges from 30 countries, bring the global total to more than 630 pledges from 140 countries, since the first conference held in Bogotá, Colombia, in 2024. The commitments mark a significant step up in global efforts to address violence against children, which affects more than 1 billion children worldwide each year.

“Violence against children is preventable and not inevitable," said Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, WHO Director-General. "And yet, many children continue to suffer harm that can affect their health and well-being throughout their lives. WHO commends the governments making commitments to end violence against children and calls on them to fulfil those commitments by investing in proven solutions that protect children before violence occurs, while ensuring those who experience violence receive the care and support, they need.”

Every day, children experience physical, sexual or psychological violence, often in places where they should feel safe; homes, schools and communities. An estimated 400 million children under five regularly experience physical punishment and/or psychological violence at the hands of parents and caregivers. One in five women and one in seven men report having experienced sexual abuse during childhood.

Violence against children can have lifelong, damaging consequences for health and can cause immediate physical, mental and emotional harm, while also increasing the risks of chronic disease, disability and high-risk behaviours later in life. Repeated exposure to violence can compound these effects.

Evidence shows that childhood violence is associated with a 66% higher risk of depression in adulthood, twice the likelihood of adolescent pregnancy, a 34% higher risk of HIV infection and increased risks of alcohol use disorders, heart disease and diabetes. An estimated 141 000 suicides each year are attributable to childhood sexual abuse.

Co-organized by the Government of the Philippines, WHO and UNICEF, the conference will bring together representatives from 130 governments and more than 1300 participants, including children, young people, survivors and civil society, to accelerate prevention, investment and accountability. The meeting will focus on scaling up proven approaches, including supporting parents and caregivers, making schools safer, and strengthening health and child protection services. It will also address emerging threats, including violence facilitated by digital technologies and heightened risks during humanitarian emergencies and climate-related crises.

WHO is calling on countries to increase sustained investment in prevention, strengthen accountability and ensure that children experiencing violence receive timely, appropriate support. Health systems have a critical role in identifying abuse, providing safe, child-centred care and connecting children with protection services.

## 2. Institute of Philanthropy and WHO Launch Partnership to Advance Healthy Ageing in the Western Pacific
**Source:** WHO Mongolia  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/mongolia/news/detail-wpro/22-10-2026-institute-of-philanthropy-and-who-launch-partnership-to-advance-healthy-ageing-in-the-western-pacific  

The Institute of Philanthropy and the World Health Organization (WHO) Regional Office for the Western Pacific are deepening their partnership on healthy ageing. At its centre is a new four-year healthy ageing initiative involving WHO and The Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK) to advance approaches that enable older people to maintain functional ability and live well in their communities. Supported by the Institute and The Hong Kong Jockey Club Charities Trust through a contribution of approximately US$ 7 million, the initiative forms part of a wider programme of work tied to the UN Decade of Healthy Ageing (2021–2030).

The initiative comprises two complementary components:
- In Hong Kong SAR (China), CUHK, working with WHO and supported by the Charities Trust, will develop and test a Multi-component Healthy Ageing Package.
- A regional component supported by the Institute will develop adaptable guidance and tools, work with interested Member States on country adaptation and demonstration and strengthen regional learning and evidence generation.

Starting in 2027, the collaboration will generate evidence, develop regional guidance on healthy ageing, support countries to adapt it to their own contexts, and strengthen regional learning. Participants at the high-level technical meeting shared experience and practical lessons on prevention, primary health care, community-based and integrated care, the health and care workforce, and stronger links between health and social support.

Population ageing is accelerating across the Region. More than 245 million people in the Western Pacific are aged 65 or older, and that number is projected to double by 2050. But healthy life expectancy has not kept pace: the gap between life expectancy and healthy life expectancy widened from 9.1 years in 2000 to 10.0 years in 2023.

WHO’s Regional Action Plan on Healthy Ageing in the Western Pacific provides a framework for helping older people maintain functional ability and live well in their communities. It emphasizes prevention and health promotion, primary health care, integrated health and social care, community-based support and enabling environments.

## 3. WHO Western Pacific Member States Agree to Urgently Accelerate Progress on Key Health Priorities
**Source:** WHO Mongolia  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/mongolia/news/detail-wpro/22-10-2026-who-western-pacific-member-states-agree-to-urgently-accelerate-progress-on-key-health-priorities  

Health leaders from across the World Health Organization (WHO) Western Pacific Region concluded their annual meeting after endorsing action plans to address lagging progress on hypertension, tobacco control and immunization, and adopting guidelines for the safe and effective use of artificial intelligence in health care.

Key priority areas endorsed by Member States:
- **Artificial Intelligence in Health:** Endorsed *Guidance for Responsible AI in Health in the Western Pacific Region* that countries can use in designing and rolling out AI safely and effectively in health systems according to specific country needs and capacities.
- **Hypertension Control:** Hypertension impacts one in three adults in the Region and is a silent killer, often masked until severe complications arise, including cardiovascular disease, stroke, disability and premature death. Member States endorsed the *Regional Plan for Accelerating Hypertension Control in the Western Pacific*, calling for integrating hypertension prevention and control as core functions of people-centred primary health care.
- **Immunization Progress:** At a time when measles and other vaccine-preventable diseases are making a comeback, Member States endorsed the *Regional Plan to Accelerate Immunization Progress in the Western Pacific* to strengthen national immunization systems and ensure equitable access to immunization for all.
- **Tobacco Control:** Tobacco causes an estimated 3 million deaths annually in the Region. Member States endorsed *Accelerating Progress towards the 2030 Tobacco Control Target in the Western Pacific Region* to combat tobacco industry interference and aggressive marketing of new products, including e-cigarettes and nicotine pouches, to young people.

## 4. Mongolia Strengthens Multisectoral Action to Accelerate Clean Household Energy Transition
**Source:** WHO Mongolia  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/mongolia/news/detail/03-11-2026-mongolia-strengthens-multisectoral-action-to-accelerate-clean-household-energy-transition  

ULAANBAATAR, 3 November 2026 – Mongolia brought together high-level government leaders and key stakeholders to advance coordinated efforts towards a clean household energy transition. The National Forum on a Stepwise Approach to Phasing Out Household Coal Use was jointly led by the Ministries of Health; Environment and Climate Change; Urban Development, Construction and Housing; and Energy, and the Governor’s Office of the Capital City, with support from WHO.

At the Forum, the ministries, together with WHO, UNDP and UNICEF, launched the *Call for Action for Clean Energy for Health*, advancing the proposed *Roadmap for a Stepwise Approach to Phasing Out Household Coal Use, 2027–2040*.

**Health Impacts and Current Situation in Mongolia:**
- Household coal use remains a major source of air pollution, particularly in the ger areas of Ulaanbaatar, provincial centres and rural soum centres.
- In 2021, the combined effects of ambient and household air pollution were associated with an estimated 2,895 deaths in Mongolia. Children, pregnant women, older people, and people living with chronic conditions face particularly serious health risks.
- Access to cleaner household energy remains unequal: an estimated 76% of urban residents use clean fuels and technologies for cooking, compared with only 22% of rural residents.

**Targets and Practical Measures:**
- The Government aims to reduce air pollution by approximately 30% in 2025–2026 and completely phase out solid fuel use in Ulaanbaatar by 2040.
- Measures include establishing smoke-free zones in major urban centres, reducing raw coal use, expanding electric and liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) heating, improving housing insulation, and ger-area redevelopment.
- To date, 5,000 households in areas with high levels of air pollution have transitioned to LPG heating, 408 households have been connected to solar energy, and 31 coal-fired boiler facilities have been converted to gas and electric heating.

## 5. Mongolia Interrupts Outbreak-Associated Measles Transmission
**Source:** WHO Mongolia  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/mongolia/news/detail/17-11-2026-mongolia-interrupts-outbreak-associated-measles-transmission  

Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, 17 November 2026 – The Ministry of Health of Mongolia and the World Health Organization (WHO) announced that transmission associated with the recent measles outbreak has been interrupted.

According to national surveillance data, the last confirmed measles case was reported on 24 August 2026, and no new measles cases have been detected since then. However, surveillance will continue, and this does not imply that the risk of measles has been eliminated completely, as imported cases remain possible.

**Key Public Health Guidance & Takeaways:**
- **Vaccination:** Timely receipt of both recommended measles-containing vaccine doses protects individuals and communities, prevents severe illness and deaths, and stops outbreaks and transmission.
- **Closing Immunity Gaps:** Despite strong progress, some populations, including children, remain unvaccinated or under-vaccinated and therefore at risk.
- **Action for Parents and Caregivers:** Parents and caregivers are encouraged to check their children’s vaccination status with their local health centre and arrange any missed doses promptly.

## 6. Mongolia Conducts Evaluation for HPV Vaccines Roll-Out
**Source:** WHO Mongolia  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/mongolia/news/feature-stories/detail-wpro/mongolia-conducts-evaluation-for-hpv-vaccines-roll-out  

Mongolia introduced HPV vaccination in 2024 as part of its cancer elimination programme. By the first quarter of 2026, more than 177,000 children aged 11–14 years have received HPV vaccination.

The Ministry of Health, with support from WHO, conducted a Post-Introduction Evaluation (PIE) to assess implementation progress, identify challenges and generate recommendations:
- **Schedule & Policy:** Adoption of a simple one-dose schedule for both girls and boys, supported by strong collaboration between the health and education sectors.
- **Catch-Up Vaccination:** Targeted follow-up efforts and community outreach successfully increased uptake in areas that initially reported low coverage.
- **Challenges Identified:** Misinformation and anti-vaccine narratives on social media, limited engagement of some private schools, and weak microplanning in certain areas.
- **Recommendations:** Sustain investment in demand generation, social listening, misinformation management, and parent engagement, while positioning schools as a platform for life-course immunization throughout childhood and adolescence.

## 7. Strengthening Mental Health Resilience in the Face of Climate Shocks Across the Western Pacific
**Source:** WHO Mongolia  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/mongolia/news/feature-stories/detail-wpro/strengthening-mental-health-resilience-in-the-face-of-climate-shocks-across-the-western-pacific  

As climate change intensifies globally, severe storms, floods, droughts, extreme temperatures and other climate-related hazards are affecting lives, livelihoods and communities across the WHO Western Pacific Region. Beyond physical destruction, these hazards have a significant impact on mental health and well-being.

**Key Facts & Recommendations:**
- Individuals face distress, anxiety, grief, uncertainty and disruptions to social support systems following emergencies, displacement or loss of livelihoods.
- Vulnerable groups—including women, children and adolescents, older persons, persons with disabilities, and communities repeatedly exposed to disasters—face heightened risks.
- The Western Pacific Region is home to more than 200 million people living with mental health conditions.
- WHO convened the Technical Consultation to Promote Mental Health Amid Climate Change and Disasters in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia (15–17 September 2026), bringing together experts from Malaysia, Mongolia, Palau, the Philippines and Vanuatu.
- Countries are working to integrate mental health and psychosocial support into climate adaptation, disaster risk reduction, and emergency preparedness strategies, while strengthening workforce capacity and community resilience.
